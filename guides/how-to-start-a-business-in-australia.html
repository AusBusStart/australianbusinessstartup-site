How to start a business in Australia

There is no single form that starts a business. Most people work through the same decisions: structure, name, ABN, tax, and whatever licence the work needs. This is that path, with the government pages the rules come from.

1Am I ready, and is this a business?

Not every idea is a business in the way the tax system means it. business.gov.au's guide to starting a business asks you to check that you are ready, and to decide whether the activity is a business or a hobby, before you register anything. A hobby is not entitled to an ABN. That guide also covers whether you are a contractor or an employee. That is a different question, and getting it wrong has tax consequences.

Write down who it is for, how you will get paid, and what it will cost to run for the first few months. That same guide has a start-up costs checklist, and it says to check the numbers with an accountant or other financial professional. This page does not list "typical" costs. They vary too much to be honest.

2Which structure do I look at first?

The structure is how the law sees the business. The three people usually compare first are sole trader, partnership and company. A trust is a further option, and it is more complicated. The choice changes your tax, your paperwork, and whether your personal assets are exposed if something goes wrong.

Read the sole trader, partnership or company comparison, then business.gov.au's business structures pages. You can change later. A change can mean cancelling an ABN and applying for a new one, so a short conversation with an accountant is worth it before you lock a company in. Starting simple is still a valid choice.

3Do I need an ABN?

An Australian Business Number is the 11-digit number other businesses and government agencies use to identify the business. It does not replace your tax file number. Registering through the Australian Business Register is free.

You get one if you are carrying on a business or other enterprise, not just because you want one. The steps, the timing and the "no renewal fee" point are in how to apply for an ABN.

You can apply on the Business Registration Service, which can take an ABN together with a business name, GST and some other registrations. business.gov.au's page is Register for an Australian Business Number. The register itself is abr.gov.au.

4Do I need to register a business name?

A business name is the name you trade under. It is not a company, a trade mark or a website address.

ASIC says you must register a business name if you carry on a business in Australia and you are not trading under your own name. A sole trader using only their first name and surname does not register that name. Add a word, such as "plumbing" or "& Co", and you do. The detail and the current fee are in how to register a business name.

5What about tax, GST and a bank account?

Business income is taxable whether or not you are registered for GST. A sole trader reports it in their individual tax return. A partnership lodges a partnership return, and each partner pays tax on their share. A company lodges its own return. The ATO sets this out on its business structures and tax obligations page.

GST is a separate registration. Many small businesses do not have to register until GST turnover reaches $75,000. Ride-sourcing and some other activities have to register from the start. Read GST registration basics before you assume either way, and check the ATO's registering for GST page.

business.gov.au says a sole trader does not have to open a separate business bank account, and recommends one so the records are easier. A company's money belongs to the company, so keep it separate from personal money.

6Which licences and insurance do I need?

This is the step that depends most on what you do. A cafe, a builder, a migration agent and an online shop do not share a licence list. Some activities are regulated by a state or territory, some by a council, and some by a Commonwealth agency.

Use business.gov.au's licences and permits tool, then check the agency that regulates your industry. Do not treat a generic checklist, including this one, as your licence list.

Insurance works the same way. If you employ anyone, workers' compensation is part of being an employer. Other cover, such as public liability or professional indemnity, depends on the work and on any licence or contract that requires it. Ask a licensed insurer or broker, and read the licence conditions.

7What records do I keep, and what if I hire?

Keep records from the first sale. business.gov.au says a sole trader has to keep financial records for at least five years. Invoices, receipts and the bank records you would need to explain your tax return belong in that set.

If you hire, you take on employer obligations: lawful pay, tax withheld from wages, superannuation, and workers' compensation. From 1 July 2026, business.gov.au says employers pay super at the same time as wages (Payday Super), rather than quarterly. Pay rates and awards are on the Fair Work Ombudsman site. Hiring is its own topic. This page only flags that the obligations start when the person starts.

8Where can I get free help?

business.gov.au lists a support line on 13 28 46, 8 am to 6 pm Monday to Friday, for help to start and run a business and for questions about government grants and programs.

The Australian Business Startup Facebook group is for practical questions from other founders. It is not a substitute for an accountant, a lawyer or the regulator.

You do not have to finish every step on day one. You do have to avoid trading under a business name you have not registered, and avoid skipping a licence the law requires for your work.

Common questions

Is there one form that starts a business in Australia?

No. Most people work through the structure, the name, an ABN if they are entitled to one, tax, and whatever licence the work needs.

Where is the official starting guide?

business.gov.au publishes a guide to starting a business. This page follows that path and links the government pages for each step.

Where can I ask a person?

business.gov.au lists a support line on 13 28 46, 8 am to 6 pm Monday to Friday. The Australian Business Startup Facebook group is for practical questions from other founders, not a substitute for an accountant, a lawyer or the regulator.
