How to apply for an ABN

An Australian Business Number is free to register, it is not a yearly renewal, and not everyone is entitled to one. Here is the practical version, with the government pages to use.

1What is an ABN?

An Australian Business Number is a unique 11-digit number. It identifies your business to the government, to other businesses and to the public. business.gov.au is clear that it does not replace your tax file number.

That page lists what people use an ABN for: identifying the business on invoices and orders, avoiding PAYG tax being withheld from payments you receive, claiming GST credits, claiming energy grants credits, and getting an Australian (.au) domain name.

A domain name is still a separate registration through a registrar. auDA administers .au domains. Owning a domain does not give you a business name, and a business name does not give you the domain.

2Who can get an ABN?

Not everyone needs an ABN, and not everyone can have one. business.gov.au says you need to be running a business or other enterprise. A hobby does not qualify. Check entitlement on the Australian Business Register before you apply. The ATO manages that register.

The structure matters here. A sole trader applies as an individual. A partnership and a company are different entities and need their own ABN. Changing structure later usually means cancelling the old ABN and applying for a new one. The cases business.gov.au names are sole trader to company, sole trader to partnership, and partnership to company.

3How much does an ABN cost?

It is free to register for an ABN through the Australian Business Register. That is business.gov.au's wording, not a special offer.

An ABN is not on a one-year or three-year renewal the way a business name is. There is no government renewal fee to "keep your ABN current". You are entitled to it while you are running the enterprise, and the register can check that you still are. If you stop, sell, close, or stop operating in Australia, business.gov.au says you need to cancel it.

Anyone who sends an invoice to renew or maintain your ABN is not collecting a government fee. That pattern is covered in the Scam Watch summary.

4What do I need before I apply?

Have your business structure, proof of identity, and details of the business activities and the people associated with it. Those are the three things business.gov.au says to have ready. If details are missing, a review is more likely.

business.gov.au says to have three things ready before you register:

  • your business structure
  • proof of identity
  • details of your business activities and of the people associated with the business

If the application has everything it needs, you are told the outcome as soon as you finish the online form. If details are missing, it is more likely to be held for review.

5Where do I apply for an ABN?

Apply on the Australian Government Business Registration Service or the Australian Business Register. Registering is free. A private firm can lodge it for you, but any fee is for their service, not a charge from the government.

Apply on the government site, not a lookalike.

  • Business Registration Service (register.business.gov.au). This is the Australian Government service for a new ABN. You can apply for a business name, GST and some other tax registrations in the same form. The service says registering for an ABN is free.
  • Australian Business Register (abr.gov.au), which business.gov.au also points to for the application and for the entitlement check.

A private company can lodge an application for you. That is a service you choose and pay them for. It is not a fee the government charges to issue the ABN. If you use one, you should still understand what was lodged in your name.

6What happens after I apply?

If the form is complete and successful, business.gov.au says you receive the ABN when you finish. A refusal comes with a refusal number and a letter within 14 days. If it needs review, you get a reference number and the review can take 20 business days.

business.gov.au describes three outcomes:

  • Successful. You receive your ABN when you finish, if the information was complete.
  • Refused. You receive a refusal number, then a letter within 14 days with the reason.
  • Needs review. You receive a reference number. Reviews can take 20 business days, and the ATO may contact you for more information.

Your public details then appear on ABN Lookup. Use it to check your own record, and to check a supplier before you pay them.

If something changes, you have 28 days after you become aware of the change to update the register. business.gov.au points to the ABR for how to update. If you also have a business name, a change of details may need an update with ASIC as well.

Most ABN details are public. The ABR has a process for asking that some details not be disclosed, including where a sole trader has a safety concern. Read that process on abr.gov.au before you apply if it applies to you. Details can be visible while a request is being considered.

7Do I have to pay to renew an ABN?

New ABNs attract letters and emails that look official. Some are private companies offering to do a renewal you do not need. Some are straight-out invoices. The government application is free, and an ABN does not have a renewal fee.

Check the sender yourself. Do not use the phone number or link in an unexpected message. The Scam Watch summary covers the fake ABN and ASIC invoices, and the full guide is the one we email when you sign up.

Common questions

Is an ABN free?

Yes. business.gov.au says it is free to register for an ABN through the Australian Business Register. There is no yearly renewal fee.

Does an ABN replace my tax file number?

No. An ABN is the 11-digit number that identifies the business. It does not replace your tax file number.

Who can get an ABN?

You need to be running a business or other enterprise. A hobby does not qualify. Check entitlement on the Australian Business Register before you apply.

Do I have to pay a renewal invoice for my ABN?

No. An ABN is not renewed the way a business name is. An invoice that asks you to pay to renew or maintain an ABN is not a government fee.
