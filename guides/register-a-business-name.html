How do I register a business name with ASIC?

A business name is the name customers see. It is registered nationally with ASIC, it is not a company or a trade mark, and it does have a fee. Here is when you need one, and how the official application works.

1What is a business name?

A business name is simply the name the business trades under. business.gov.au describes it as the name you tell your customers. Business names are national. You do not register the same name again with each state or territory.

It is not the same thing as:

  • an ABN, which identifies the entity
  • a company, which is a separate legal structure registered with ASIC
  • a trade mark, which is how you get exclusive rights to use a name as a brand, through IP Australia
  • a domain name, which you buy from a registrar. auDA administers .au domains

Registering a business name stops another business registering an identical or nearly identical name. ASIC says it does not stop other people using similar words, and it does not protect you if the name infringes someone else's trade mark. Search existing trade marks before you pay. business.gov.au points to IP Australia's TM Checker for that.

2When do I have to register a business name?

ASIC says you must register a business name if you run a business in Australia and you are not trading under your own name. You do not register one if you are:

  • a sole trader operating under your first name and surname, and nothing else
  • a partnership operating under all of the partners' personal names
  • a registered Australian company, and the name you operate under is the company's own name

ASIC's examples: John Smith does not register "John Smith", but does register "John Smith Gardening" or "John Smith & Co". Partners Peter Nguyen and Helen Lee do not register "Peter Nguyen and Helen Lee", but do register "Nguyen & Lee Plumbing".

If you are not entitled to an ABN, you are not carrying on a business for this purpose, and you do not register a business name either. Start with the ABN guide if you are unsure the activity is a business.

3How do I check a business name is available?

Search the business names register before you apply. ASIC's availability check uses a traffic light:

  • Green. The name is available.
  • Amber. ASIC needs to assess it after you apply. That can be because the name is nearly identical to another, or because the word is unfamiliar to the system (made-up words, unusual spelling, words joined together). Those go into a queue.
  • Red. The name is unavailable.

Other laws still apply. Some state and territory laws restrict professional or industry words. ASIC says that if you register a name another law does not let you use, the responsible body can stop you using it, and ASIC will not refund the fee.

You also cannot hold a business name if you are disqualified from managing corporations, or if you have been convicted of certain offences. ASIC publishes that list on the register-a-business-name page.

4What does a business name cost?

business.gov.au publishes the ASIC fee as $47 for one year and $108 for three years, for registration or renewal. Those amounts followed the 1 July 2026 increase. ASIC indexes the fees, so confirm the figure on ASIC's fee page when you apply.

business.gov.au publishes the fees to register a business name with ASIC as:

  • $47 for 1 year
  • $108 for 3 years

Those are also the renewal fees. business.gov.au's 1 July 2026 update said the one-year fee rose from $45 to $47, and the three-year fee from $104 to $108. ASIC indexes many of its fees on 1 July using the consumer price index, and GST does not apply to ASIC fees. Check the figure on ASIC's fee indexation page when you apply. The dollar amounts above are what business.gov.au was publishing when this page was checked in October 2026.

5How do I register a business name with ASIC?

You need an ABN, or the reference number from an ABN application that is still being processed. A joint venture puts each partner's ABN on the application. You can also apply for the ABN and the business name together on the Business Registration Service.

To register with ASIC directly, use ASIC's how-to guide. In short:

  1. Create an ASIC Connect account if you do not have one. You do not need an ASIC key to register a business name.
  2. Open Licences and Registrations, choose Business name registration, and start.
  3. Enter the ABN or ABN reference number with no spaces.
  4. Enter the name exactly as you want it on the register. ASIC says business names are case sensitive, so capitals matter.
  5. Choose 1 year or 3 years, and enter the holder, the address and the eligibility declaration.
  6. Review, declare and pay.

ASIC says you must pay within 10 business days of the application being approved, or the application is refused and you have to reapply. An accountant, lawyer, friend or family member can lodge it for you as an authorised lodger. ASIC suggests they enter your contact details, not theirs, so renewal notices come to you.

You will be asked for a service-of-documents address (a street address or PO Box in Australia, and it is public), a principal place of business (a physical street address in Australia, and it is public), and an email address (not public). If you are an individual and the place of business is your home, ASIC shows only the suburb, state or territory and postcode on the public register. If safety is a concern, read ASIC's page on hiding personal details before you submit.

business.gov.au says that if the details and the fee are in order, confirmation is usually within 2 business days for a card payment and within 5 business days for BPAY, EFT or a bank transfer. ASIC says the name is registered once the application is in and the payment has cleared, which can take up to 24 hours, and the record of registration is emailed from ASIC.transactions.no-reply@asic.gov.au. Check the spam folder before you assume it failed.

6What do I do after the name is registered?

You can use the name once it appears on the register. You do not have to display the certificate. You do have to display the business name where the business is open to the public, such as a shopfront. ASIC says the name and your ABN go on business documents such as invoices and purchase orders. business.gov.au also includes quotes and other written communication.

Tell ASIC about changes to your details, usually within 28 days. Updating the holder's details is free. Cancelling or transferring is a separate transaction. A transfer uses a transfer number, and the name has to be entered exactly as it already appears on the register.

7How do renewals and fake invoices work?

business.gov.au says ASIC emails a renewal notice at least 30 days before the renewal date. You renew online through ASIC Connect, for one year or three years, at the fee on ASIC's schedule. If you do not renew, ASIC may cancel the name and someone else can register it.

Letters that arrive earlier than that, from a sender that is not ASIC, or for a much higher fee, are the usual "business name renewal" approach. Some are offers from private companies. Some are scams. You do not have to pay them. Renew with ASIC, and read the Scam Watch summary before you pay an unexpected invoice. ASIC's renewal emails come from an @asic.gov.au address. The record-of-registration mail comes from ASIC.transactions.no-reply@asic.gov.au.

Common questions

Do I need a business name if I trade under my own name?

Not if you are a sole trader using only your first name and surname, with nothing added. ASIC says adding words, such as a trade or "& Co", means you register the name.

How much is a business name?

business.gov.au lists $47 for one year and $108 for three years with ASIC, as published when this page was checked in October 2026. The fee is indexed on 1 July.

Does a business name give me exclusive rights?

It stops another business registering an identical or nearly identical name. It is not a trade mark. Exclusive brand rights come from IP Australia, not from the business name.
