Money and support

Small business grants and support in Australia

Grants exist, and most new businesses will not get one to cover their start-up costs. The useful part is knowing where the real lists are, what they are usually for, and how the fake ones ask for money.

1What a grant is, and is not

A government grant is money or support for a defined purpose, usually competitive, and usually with strings: an eligible activity, a report, and sometimes money you have to spend first. It is not a prize for registering an ABN, and it is not a loan you have already been approved for.

business.gov.au's funding page says that, in general, the government does not provide finance for starting or buying a business. Grants are more often aimed at things like research and development, expanding, innovating, or exporting. Debt and equity are the two main kinds of funding for actually starting. This page does not compare lenders or investors. That choice depends on your numbers.

This page also does not list open rounds or dollar amounts. Rounds close, guidelines change, and a list copied onto a website goes stale. Use the finder below, and read the guidelines on the agency's own site.

2Where to look, for free

Start with the Grants and programs finder on business.gov.au. It is a free search across government grants, funding and support. You answer a few questions about where you operate and your industry. business.gov.au says it takes less than five minutes, and that if nothing matches you should check back because new programs open.

The finder is national in the sense that it covers programs from across government, including state and territory ones it knows about. Your state or territory small-business agency, and sometimes your council, will have others. If a program matters to you, open the guidelines from the agency that runs it. Look for .gov.au in the address.

business.gov.au's notice on grant information (published 7 May 2024, and still the guidance on that page) says government grant information is free, and that you never need to pay for it. State, territory and federal grant information is available through official government sources. The finder is the place they tell you to start. The support line on that page is 13 28 46.

3Other free support

Grants are one slice of help. business.gov.au's help page also points to:

  • its own support team, on 13 28 46, 8 am to 6 pm Monday to Friday, including questions about grants and programs
  • workshops, events and skills topics such as planning, record keeping and intellectual property
  • Workforce Australia's self-employment assistance, which can include workshops, small-business training, help with a business plan, sessions with a business adviser, a business health check, and coaching that may include financial support for eligible businesses

Eligibility for that employment program is not something to guess from a blog. Read the current criteria on the government page before you plan around it.

None of this replaces an accountant if the question is about tax, or a lawyer if the question is about a contract or a structure. The Australian Business Startup Facebook group is useful for what other founders actually did. Treat it as experience, not as a ruling.

4Grant scams

business.gov.au's funding page says that if you get a call or email offering funding you did not apply for, it is almost certainly a scam, and you can report it to Scamwatch. The grant-information notice adds the practical tests:

  • You should not be asked to pay for access to government grant information. Be wary of sites that charge a fee to "unlock" a list.
  • Grants are awarded on merit. You cannot pay for special access or a guaranteed approval.
  • A paid grant writer can be a legitimate service. That is different from paying a stranger an upfront "release" or "processing" fee, or handing over a myGov or bank login.
  • Collect the rules from the agency's own website. Look for .gov.au. Logos and official-looking layouts are easy to copy. An address like a private .com.au with a government-sounding name is not a government site.

The same pattern shows up in our Scam Watch summary under fake grants. The longer checklist is in the guide we email to newsletter subscribers.

5Before you spend time on an application

Read the guidelines all the way through. Check the closing date, what you must already have in place (often an ABN, and sometimes GST registration, insurance, or matched funding), and what you will have to report later. If the numbers only work if the grant arrives, they do not work yet.

Keep the application in your own records, in your own login. Do not give a helper the only set of credentials for myGov, the ATO, or your bank.

If you are still at the "do I even have an ABN" stage, do that first. The start-up steps and the ABN guide are the relevant pages. A grant application is a poor reason to invent a business structure you do not understand.
