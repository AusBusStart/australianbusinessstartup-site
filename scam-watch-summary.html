Scam Watch summary

Scams targeting new Australian businesses

Registering an ABN or a business name puts your details on a public register. Most of what follows is junk. Some of it is designed to look like a bill you have to pay. This is the short public version. The full guide, with the red flags and the checklist, is what we email when you sign up.

The habit that stops most of them

Stop. Check. Then act. Do not use the phone number, link or QR code in a message you were not expecting. Look the organisation up yourself, or log in to the account you already have.

The notes below are the patterns we see aimed at new Australian businesses. Scams change. The agencies linked in the last section publish the current warnings.

1Fake renewal invoices

A letter or email says your business name, company or ABN is due for renewal. It uses your real details, because those details are public. Sometimes it is a private company offering to renew on your behalf, for more than the official fee, with "this is not an invoice" in the fine print. Sometimes it is just a scam.

An ABN is free and does not have a renewal fee. A business name does renew, with ASIC, at ASIC's published fee. Check the date by logging in to ASIC Connect, not by paying the letter. ASIC has said its own email comes from addresses ending in @asic.gov.au.

2ATO impersonation

A call threatens arrest or a fine over unpaid tax. A text says you are owed a refund. An email asks you to "verify" on a page that looks like myGov. The ATO has said it will not send an unsolicited SMS or email with a link or a QR code, or ask you to hand over personal information that way.

Hang up. Check through myGov or the ATO's own website and phone numbers. The reporting line published for ATO impersonation is 1800 008 540, and scam emails can be forwarded to ReportScams@ato.gov.au. Those contacts were checked for our full guide in October 2026. Confirm them on ato.gov.au if you are about to rely on them.

3Payment redirection

An email that looks like it came from a supplier, your bookkeeper or a customer says the bank details have changed. Sometimes the scammer is inside a real email thread. Pay the new account and the money is gone.

Make it a rule: a change of bank details is confirmed by a phone call to a number you already had, not the number in the email. Turn on multi-factor authentication for email. If you have already paid, call your bank before you do anything else.

4Domain, trade mark and directory lookalikes

Domain invoices turn up from a company that is not your registrar, often for a name that is slightly different from yours, or with a story that someone else wants the name unless you pay today. auDA, which administers .au domains, has warned about phishing that pretends to be auDA. Log in to your registrar. Do not use the link in the email.

Trade mark applications are public. Soon after you file, lookalike letters offer an "international register", an early renewal, or a fee that claims to be for WIPO. IP Australia says genuine notices go to your IP Australia account, and a listing on an unofficial register does not give you legal protection. Check ipaustralia.gov.au before you pay.

Directory scams are a phone call to "confirm your listing", followed by an invoice for an ad you never ordered. Do not say yes, and do not confirm details, on a call you did not expect.

5Fake buyers and suppliers

A buyer places a large order, "overpays", and asks you to refund the difference before their payment bounces. Or a supplier is far too cheap, takes a deposit, and disappears. Treat money as received only when it has cleared in your own account. Check a new supplier on ABN Lookup, and start with a small order.

6"Your Facebook Page will be disabled"

A message claims to be from Meta or a page-policy team. Your Page will be removed unless you appeal through a link. The link is a fake login that takes the password and the two-factor code. Check warnings from inside Facebook, using the app or the site you typed yourself. Do not use the link in the message.

7Fake grants and guaranteed funding

You are told the business has been approved for a government grant, if you pay an application or release fee, or if you share a myGov login. business.gov.au says grant information is free and grants are awarded on merit. Look programs up yourself on the Grants and programs finder. There is more on this in the grants guide.

8Cold pitches and fake jobs

Some SEO, website and lead-generation pitches are real businesses. The ones that hurt take a deposit and vanish, or register your domain and your Google profile in their name. Guaranteed rankings, no ABN, and pressure to pay a large deposit up front are the flags. Keep the domain, the website and the ad accounts in your name.

Separately, scammers post jobs in a real business's name to collect identity documents or money from applicants. If that happens to you, report the ad on the platform and tell your customers it was not you. Any "job" that asks the worker to pay in order to earn is a scam, including offers that arrive in your own inbox.

Where to report

If money has left your account, call your bank first, using the number on the card or on the bank's own website.

  • Scamwatch, run by the National Anti-Scam Centre, takes reports of scams even when you did not lose money.
  • ReportCyber (Australian Cyber Security Centre) is for cybercrime such as a hacked email account or payment redirection. The 24/7 line published for this is 1300 292 371 (1300 CYBER1).
  • The ATO impersonation line published for this is 1800 008 540. Forward scam emails to ReportScams@ato.gov.au.
  • ASIC publishes warnings about scams impersonating ASIC. You can also check a company on ASIC's registers before you deal with it.
  • IDCARE provides free identity and cyber support. The numbers published for this are 1800 595 160 for individuals and 1800 595 170 for small businesses, via idcare.org.

Phone numbers change. If a number on this page and the number on the agency's website ever disagree, use the website. These contacts match our full guide, which was checked in October 2026.
